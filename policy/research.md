# Evidence-based research

Use when researching external claims, products, standards, prices, limits or architecture guidance that affects a software decision. Follows the SDLC policy.

1. Fetch and read primary sources in full; search snippets are not evidence. Prefer raw evidence: source code, specifications, measured results, reproducible commands, externally checkable acceptance criteria.
2. Check version-sensitive facts against the product and version actually in use. Give retrieval dates for prices, plan limits and mutable policies.
3. Support each load-bearing claim with two independent authoritative sources when practical. State conflicts, and label each claim verified (retrieved and read) or told (reported by a source).
4. Put unsupported or unavailable facts in a Gaps list. A failed retrieval is a tool limit, not proof the fact does not exist: try another route before concluding.
5. Never invent numbers, and never upgrade a pilot, tabletop review or vendor claim into production certification.
6. End with a short Sources list for anything that can be linked.
